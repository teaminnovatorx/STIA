# PHASE 01: THE DATA VACUUM ANALYSIS
## Empirical Research into the Fragmented AMR Landscape of Sub-Saharan Africa (2024-2026)

### 1. The Global vs. Local Disconnect
Current global AMR surveillance systems, such as the WHO’s GLASS, rely on a "Top-Down" approach. This assumes that patients enter a formal hospital, a sample is taken, a lab tests it, and the data is uploaded. In Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), this chain breaks at the first link.

**Empirical Finding:** Research from late 2024 indicates that over **70% of clinical encounters** in countries like Nigeria and Kenya occur in the informal sector. These patients never enter the "Formal Chain," making their infections invisible to global surveillance.

### 2. Mortality Statistics: The Silent Pandemic
- **Current Burden (2025):** 27.3 deaths per 100,000—the highest in the world.
- **Neonatal Crisis:** In West Africa, nearly **31% of neonatal sepsis deaths** are now linked to multidrug-resistant (MDR) bacteria.
- **The Proportional Threat:** AMR now kills more people in SSA annually than the combined total of HIV/AIDS and Malaria.

### 3. The "Diagnostic Desert"
The lack of microbiology laboratory capacity is the primary driver of empiric (guesswork-based) prescribing.
- **Statistic:** Only **1.3% of health facilities** in the MAAP (Mapping Antimicrobial Resistance and Antimicrobial Use Partnership) study area had the capacity to perform bacteriology testing.
- **Outcome:** This forced "empiric guessing" is the primary selection pressure for resistant strains.

### 4. Conclusion
The "Data Vacuum" is not a lack of data; it is a lack of **integration**. The data exists in the notes of community health workers and the ledgers of informal pharmacists. SENTRI’s primary research goal is to bridge this gap.
